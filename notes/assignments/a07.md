## Objective

Set the CG limits of your aircraft, size the horizontal and vertical tails and the control surfaces, and {term}`trim` the aircraft at every condition your {term}`RFP` names. The inputs are the A04 loft, the A05 propulsion and the A06 {term}`design point`. This assignment covers the calendar's A10 and A11. Dynamic stability comes in a later assignment.

You will:

1. Build the weight statement and the CG table, and draw the {term}`CG envelope`.
2. Size the horizontal tail with the {term}`scissor plot` and set the forward and aft CG limits.
3. Trim the aircraft at every condition and report the trim drag.
4. Size the vertical tail and choose the dihedral.
5. Size the elevator, the rudder and the ailerons, and define the flight control system.
6. Check the hand results with a vortex-lattice model and update the three-view.
7. Defend the CG limits and the tail sizes.

All deliverables are required. Every input comes from {term}`ASORT` or carries a citation.

**AI tools.** Do the work yourselves first. An AI tool may then check or extend a draft. Restate what you adopt in your own words, with its source, and put every prompt and response in the deck appendix. Do not use weights, CG positions or aerodynamic derivatives from an AI tool.

## Deliverable 1: Weight statement, CG table and CG envelope

1. Make the weight statement with the groups your RFP lists under Weights, and the roll-up from empty weight to {term}`MTOW` with pilots and fuel. State the source of each weight: the sizing code, the component weight relations of the mass-properties lecture or a cited value.
2. Make the CG table: one row per item with its weight, its position from the nose taken from the 3DEXPERIENCE model, and its moment. Compute the empty weight and its CG.
3. Define the loading cases: one or two pilots, each from 103 to 205 lb plus 35 lb of flight suit, equipment and survival kit; fuel from full to empty. Compute the CG of each case.
4. State the tank arrangement and the burn sequence. Compute the CG travel along each design mission as fuel burns, from the A03 segment tables.
5. Draw the CG envelope: weight against CG position as a fraction of the mean aerodynamic chord, with every loading case and the CG travel of each mission. Draw it first without limits. Add the forward and aft limits when Deliverable 2 gives them.
6. Enter the empty weight and its CG in ASORT.

## Deliverable 2: Horizontal tail and CG limits

1. State the inputs of the scissor plot: the wing-body aerodynamic center with its fuselage shift, $C_{M,ac}^{W}$, $C_{L\alpha}^{W}$, the downwash gradient, $\eta_H$, the tail arm $l_t$ and the main gear position from the 3DEXPERIENCE model, and the tail aspect ratio, elevator chord ratio and elevator deflection limit you start from. State the sign conventions.
2. Choose the static margin. State the MIL-STD-1797 class and level you design to and the class values you compared it with. If you choose a negative static margin, state the flight control system it requires.
3. Draw the scissor plot, tail area ratio $S_t/S$ against CG position, with the lecture's four lines (slide 17), each with its own flap setting:
   - Line 1, stability at the chosen static margin.
   - Line 2, stall recovery at the aft CG.
   - Line 3, takeoff rotation at $V_R$: MTOW, 5,000 ft {term}`density altitude`, takeoff flaps, forward CG.
   - Line 4, landing approach: {term}`MLW`, landing flaps, forward CG.
4. Code the four lines as a module of the sizing code that reads ASORT. Deliverables 3 and 4 add the trim solve and the fin build-up to it.
5. Mark the loading range of Deliverable 1 on the plot. Select $S_t/S$. Read the forward and aft limits and state the margin between the loading range and each limit. If the range does not fit, change the loading, the wing position or the tail arm and repeat Deliverable 1. State the change.

6. Define the tail geometry: aspect ratio, taper ratio, sweep, airfoil, thickness, span and chords. Compare $V_H$ with the lecture's class value.
7. Compute the neutral point and the static margin at the empty weight, at every loading case, along each design mission and at both Mach numbers of the maximum speed requirement, 0.78 at {term}`threshold` and 0.90 at {term}`objective`, with the compressibility correction you use. Enter the limits and the tail geometry in ASORT.

## Deliverable 3: Trim

1. Compute the elevator derivatives $C_{L\delta_e}$ and $C_{M\delta_e}$ from $\tau$ and the elevator chord ratio of Deliverable 2. Correct $C_{L\alpha}^{T}$ and $\tau$ to the Mach number of each condition.
2. Set the tail incidence so that the elevator deflection is zero at the condition you choose. State the condition.
3. Make the trim table (slide 29), one row per condition:
   - Maximum speed at Mach 0.78 at threshold and 0.90 at objective: 25,000 ft, MTOW less 50% fuel, both CG limits.
   - Your cruise condition, both CG limits.
   - Climb, at a point of each A03 climb segment you state, both CG limits.
   - Sustained turn at +4.0 g at threshold and +6.0 g at objective: 15,000 ft, MTOW less 50% fuel, both CG limits, with the elevator deflection per g.
   - Takeoff rotation at $V_R$: MTOW, 5,000 ft density altitude, takeoff flaps, forward CG, with the elevator deflection needed to rotate.
   - Approach and landing: MLW, 5,000 ft density altitude, landing flaps, forward CG, on the 3.5° {term}`FCLP` glide slope, with the pitch attitude.
   - With two engines, the engine-out condition of Deliverable 4.
4. Record in each row the trim lift coefficient, the trim angle of attack, the elevator deflection against the limit you state, the tail lift coefficient against the tail stall, and the trim drag. Include the thrust pitching moment in every row.
5. Draw the trim diagram: elevator deflection and angle of attack against trim lift coefficient, one line per CG limit and flap configuration, with the elevator limit and the points of step 3. Read the slowest trimmable speed at the forward CG and compare it with the stall and approach speeds.
6. Compute the trimmed polar of the cruise configuration at the forward CG. State the change in lift-to-drag ratio at the cruise condition from the A05 polar. Enter the trimmed polar in ASORT.
7. Name the row with the largest elevator deflection.

## Deliverable 4: Vertical tail and dihedral

1. Size the vertical tail from the lecture's class volume coefficient first. Then compute $C_{n\beta}$ as the sum of the fin, fuselage and wing-sweep contributions and compare it with the lecture's target range (slide 32). State the sidewash and dynamic-pressure factor you use.
2. Run the three checks (slide 34):
   - Engine failure, with two engines: at the $V_{mc}$ you determine, aft CG, with the drag of the dead engine, the rudder inside the limit you state and the bank angle you allow. With one engine, state that the check does not apply. Enter $V_{mc}$ in ASORT.
   - Crosswind landing: the crosswind your {term}`certification basis` requires.
   - Spin recovery: a side-view sketch of the rudder area outside the horizontal tail wake, with the wake boundaries of Gudmundsson or Raymer.
3. Resize the fin if a check fails.
4. Compute $C_{\ell\beta}$ from the wing position, the sweep and the dihedral. Choose the dihedral angle that puts $C_{\ell\beta}$ inside the lecture's target range (slide 32).
5. State the MIL-STD-1797 level the crosswind and engine-out results meet.
6. Enter the fin geometry, $l_v$ and the dihedral in ASORT.

## Deliverable 5: Control surfaces and the flight control system

1. Set the elevator chord ratio and span from the Deliverable 3 row with the largest deflection, with a flare margin you state. Repeat the Deliverable 3 rows the change affects.
2. Set the rudder chord ratio and span from the Deliverable 4 check that needs the larger rudder.
3. State the roll requirement you use, from MIL-STD-1797 for your class and level. Size the ailerons for it at the slowest condition with flaps down, with the deflection margin the lecture states (slide 39). Report the inboard and outboard span stations and the chord ratio. If the high-lift system occupies the aileron span, state the roll control device you use and size it.
4. Make the control surface table: surface, sizing condition, chord ratio, span stations, area, deflection limits and the trim device.
5. Define the flight control system: mechanical, powered or fly-by-wire, with an actuator per surface and, if the static margin is negative, the stability augmentation it needs. Draw one block diagram and place the actuators and any flight control computers in the 3DEXPERIENCE model.

## Deliverable 6: Vortex-lattice check and three-view

1. Build a vortex-lattice model of the A04 wing and the tails of Deliverables 2 and 4, in AVL, XFLR5 or OpenVSP. Report $C_{L\alpha}$, $C_{M\alpha}$, the neutral point, $C_{n\beta}$, $C_{\ell\beta}$ and the control derivatives at the cruise condition, and the downwash gradient from tail-on and tail-off runs.
2. Compare each value with the result of Deliverables 2 to 5 in one table. State the cause of each difference larger than the lecture's threshold (slide 41).
3. State the values ASORT holds after the comparison. Redraw the scissor plot and recompute the trim table where a value changed.
4. Run the sizing code again with the tail weights and the trimmed polar. Report the change in empty weight, takeoff weight and CG. State whether the A06 design point is still inside the {term}`feasible region` and the CG inside the limits. If not, state the change needed and stop there.
5. Draw the three-view with the neutral point, the tail moment arms, the control surface sizes and the table of areas. Submit the updated 3DEXPERIENCE model.

## Deliverable 7: Defense and reflection

Present the design defense on two slides:

1. The forward and aft CG limits, the loading range and the margin to each limit. The tail areas with the line or check that sizes each.
2. The trim row that sizes the elevator, and the trim drag at cruise.
3. The change in tail area and in the CG limits when one input changes, the static margin or a loading case.
4. The hand calculation and vortex-lattice result that differ most, and the data that would settle the difference. The requirement least likely to be met.

Any member may be asked to present any slide.

Each member writes a reflection of at most 150 words. Name the limit, surface or check you contributed most to and how you justified it. Name the result you would check first if the empty CG moved aft. Add one sentence naming the AI tools you used and for what.
