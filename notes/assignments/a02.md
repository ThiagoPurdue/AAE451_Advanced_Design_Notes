## Objective

This assignment establishes the {term}`certification basis` for your aircraft, builds a database of historical aircraft, and produces a first takeoff-weight estimate against your A01 {term}`design mission`. The work uses AI tools under the rules below and is graded with the course AI-Assisted Homework Rubric.

You will:

1. Establish your certification basis and add the resulting requirements to your A01 set.
2. Build a team database of historical aircraft, with a source for every value.
3. Fit an empty-weight trend and state how your aircraft departs from it.
4. Compute a first takeoff-weight estimate for your A01 design mission.
5. Work with a model at each step, and record what its input changed in your design.
6. Defend the converged weight and carry its open risks into A03.

Your team keeps the Navy Jet Trainer {term}`RFP` from A01. All deliverables are required.

:::{seealso}
The methods are in Lecture 2: the empty-weight trend lines and regression in [](../lecture-02/weight-estimation.md); the segment fractions, the Breguet equations and the sizing loop in [](../lecture-02/mission-fuel.md); and a loop you can run in [](../lecture-02/sizing-loop.ipynb).
:::

## How AI is used in this assignment

1. Draft each deliverable yourselves. Date the draft before a model sees it. A deliverable drafted by a model receives no credit.
2. Deliverables 1 through 4 each end with a working session. Give the model the dated draft, your RFP, your requirements, and your data. Ask it to critique the draft and to extend the work as the deliverable states.
3. Run each session when its draft is ready, not at the end of the assignment.
4. Before adopting a suggestion: restate it in your own words, state why it applies to your mission and requirements, and cite its source. A suggestion without a source is recorded as a team assumption.
5. The team makes the design decisions and defends them in its own words. "The AI said so" is not a justification.
6. Put every prompt and every full model return in the deck appendix.
7. Concept and tool questions are allowed at any time, such as what the range equation assumes or how to fit a curve in MATLAB.

## Deliverable 1: Certification basis

The certification basis for the Navy Jet Trainer is MIL-HDBK-516C. The handbook is posted on Brightspace.

1. Build one table in the deck: each section of the handbook that applies to your design, what it demands, and whether your A01 requirements already cover it. Name the member who worked each block of rows. Date the table.
2. Write the missing requirements into `TeamXX_A02_Requirements.slreqx`. Use "The system shall..." form. Cite the document and section in `Rationale`. Assign a `Verification Method`.
3. Trace each new {term}`requirement` to a stakeholder {term}`need`. If no need covers airworthiness, add one to `TeamXX_A02_Needs.slreqx`. An untraced certification requirement is gold plating under the A01 rules.
4. Identify where the handbook does not cover your aircraft. Record the gap in the table and state what you would propose to the certifying authority.
5. Mark the sections that set a design weight or a performance floor, such as stall speed or a climb gradient. These become checks on your Deliverable 4 result and on A03.
6. Working session. Give the model the dated table and your requirement set. Ask it to critique the table, sections missed and sections misread, and to state which sections it expects to constrain your design most. Adopt under the rules above; cite the document section for every row you change.
7. Re-export `TeamXX_A02_Traceability.pdf`. The A01 checks apply: no orphan needs and no gold plating, or a written justification for each one kept.

Start from the aircraft below, the trainer cohort shown in [Lecture 3](#trainer-cohort). The list is a starting point, not a boundary:

Collect at least these parameters for every aircraft you keep:

:::{table} Parameters to collect, and what each one feeds.

| Parameter | What it feeds |
| --- | --- |
| Takeoff gross weight | The trend and the sizing loop |
| Empty weight | The trend |

Requirements for `TeamXX_A02_Database.xlsx`:

- One row per aircraft. One column per parameter. One source column.
- Cite a source for every value. Mark a value unverified if no source exists, and exclude it from the fit.
- Name the member who collected each row. Date the sheet.

Working session. Give the model the dated sheet and your mission. Ask it to cross-check your values against published sources, to flag each discrepancy with the source it used, and to name relevant aircraft you did not consider. Change a value only after checking the source yourself. Add an aircraft only with your own stated relevance and sourced data.

## Deliverable 3: Empty-weight trend

1. Plot empty-weight fraction against takeoff gross weight from your database.
2. Fit a trend line. Report the R-squared. State whether the trend is real. A least-squares fit returns coefficients whether or not a relationship exists; {numref}`fig-a02-trend` shows the failure.
3. State how your aircraft departs from the trend. Apply an adjustment, the {term}`technology factor` of Lecture 2, from a citable source (a NASA report, an AIAA paper, a manufacturer publication) or label it as a team assumption. State the era and structure material of your database before using its coefficients.
4. Choose the empty-weight relation for Deliverable 4: this fitted trend, or Raymer's regression for your class using the Deliverable 2 parameters. State the choice and the reason.
5. Working session. Give the model the dated plot, the fit, and your adjustment argument. Ask it to critique both against published trends for your class, and to propose explanations for the scatter you see. Adopt under the rules above. The relation choice in step 4 stays the team's.

## Deliverable 4: First weight sizing

1. Divide your A01 design mission into segments.
2. Assign a weight fraction to each segment. Use the Breguet range equation for cruise segments and the endurance equation for loiter segments. Use published historical fractions for the remaining segments. Cite a source for each fraction.
3. Use the reserve requirements stated in the RFP. Do not substitute generic defaults.
4. Iterate takeoff weight until the empty-weight relation chosen in Deliverable 3, the mission fuel, and the RFP payload agree. Submit the MATLAB code as `TeamXX_A02_Sizing.zip`. The code must run on another member's machine.
5. Check the converged weight and mission against the certification sections marked in Deliverable 1. Record each check as pass, fail, or not yet checkable. Carry the open checks to A03.
6. Present one slide: mission segments, weight fraction per segment with source, converged takeoff weight, the certification checks, and the sensitivity of takeoff weight to the Deliverable 3 adjustment. Results without a sensitivity analysis are incomplete.
7. Working session. Give the model the dated mission table and the converged result, with your {term}`CONOPS` and RFP. Ask it to critique the segment fractions and reserves, and to name the inputs it expects the converged weight to be most sensitive to. Test the named inputs you had not considered and report them with the step 6 sensitivity.

:::{warning}
The sizing loop in Lecture 2 sizes an invented small transport. Its mission, its segment fractions and its trend line show the method. Yours come from the RFP, your A01 mission and your database.
:::

## Deliverable 5: AI use record

Two slides.

1. List the working sessions: the deliverable, the tool, the date, and who ran it.
2. List what you adopted or adapted, each restated in your own words, with its source or its team-assumption label, and what it changed in the deliverable.
3. List what you rejected, with the engineering reason.
4. Show the exchange that most improved your result: what you asked, what the model returned, your judgment, and the change.

A session that changed nothing is a valid result. State why it changed nothing.

The record is graded against the prompts and returns in the appendix. A session with no documented model return receives no credit.

## Deliverable 6: Design defense, disclosure and reflection

**Design defense.** Two slides. This material feeds your {term}`SRR` initial-sizing rationale in week 5.

1. Plot your converged takeoff weight as a labeled point on the Deliverable 3 trend, among the database aircraft. State whether your design point sits on, above, or below the trend. State why.
2. State the two inputs your takeoff weight is most sensitive to, from the Deliverable 4 sensitivity. For each, state what evidence would change it.
3. State which requirement in your A02 set the converged weight puts most at risk, and what you will do about it in A03.

Any member presents any slide on request. Text that mirrors model phrasing scores as Developing, regardless of content.

**Individual reflection.** Each member submits 150 to 250 words answering:

1. Name the exchange that most improved your result. State what made the model's input specific to your aircraft rather than generic.
2. Name one decision the team made without the model. State why it had to be yours.
3. What will you change about how you use a model in A03?

Add one paragraph disclosing the tools used, their purpose, and what was not AI-generated.
