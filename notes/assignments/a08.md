## Objective

Compute the performance of your aircraft at every condition the {term}`RFP` names and compare each result with its requirement. The RFP names six performance requirements, two missions with their reserves, and performance data items 1 to 5. Each deliverable opens with the RFP item it answers. The inputs are the A05 installed {term}`engine deck`, the A06 {term}`design point`, the A07 weights and trimmed cruise polar, and the A04 takeoff and landing polars, all from {term}`ASORT`. Use the models of the sizing code. Do not build a second model for this assignment.

You will:

1. Set the speed limits and the limit load factor, and compute the speeds the missions use.
2. Compute both {term}`design missions <design mission>` segment by segment.
3. Compute climb and {term}`specific excess power`, and draw the flight envelope.
4. Draw the turn-rate chart at the condition of the sustained load factor requirement.
5. Compute the takeoff, critical field and landing distances.
6. Draw the maneuver V-n diagram and compute the excess power of the training flight's basic maneuvers.
7. Make the compliance table, the threshold-to-objective trades and the sizing update.
8. Defend the results.

All deliverables are required. Every input comes from ASORT or carries a citation. State the weight, altitude and engine {term}`rating` of every result. Use the International Standard Atmosphere, as the RFP requires.

**AI tools.** Do the work yourselves first. An AI tool may then check or extend a draft. Restate what you adopt in your own words, with its source, and put every prompt and response in the deck appendix. Do not use performance values, load factors or speeds from an AI tool.

## Deliverable 1: Speed limits, limit load factor and speeds

The RFP requires a maximum operating speed of Mach 0.78 at {term}`threshold` and 0.90 at {term}`objective` at 25,000 ft, {term}`MTOW` less 50% fuel. Its data item 3 asks for the maximum dive speed. The reserves of both missions are at the maximum endurance speed. The RFP does not specify the ferry cruise speed.

1. Code a performance module of the sizing code that reads ASORT: drag from the A07 trimmed cruise polar and the A04 takeoff and landing polars, with the A04 drag rise extended over the Mach range of step 3 by the method you cite, and thrust from the A05 installed engine deck. Deliverables 2 to 6 use it.
2. State the maximum operating speed and Mach number, the maximum dive speed and the positive limit load factor you design to. The RFP requires certification to MIL-HDBK-516C, which refers to JSSG-2006 for the loads. Cite the paragraph you use for each. Enter these values in ASORT. Deliverables 3, 4 and 6 use them.
3. Plot drag and thrust available against Mach number at 25,000 ft, MTOW less 50% fuel, at the rating you state. Read the maximum level-flight Mach number and compare it with 0.78 at threshold and 0.90 at objective.
4. At the ferry cruise altitude you choose in Deliverable 2 and the weight at the start of the ferry cruise, report the speed for $(L/D)_{max}$ and the best-range speed (slide 10). Deliverable 2 step 4 marks them beside the cruise speed you chose.
5. Find the maximum endurance speed of the cruise configuration as the speed of minimum fuel flow from the engine deck. Compare its lift coefficient with the lecture's optimum lift coefficients (slide 11).

## Deliverable 2: Design missions

The RFP requires internal fuel for two missions, the training flight and the ferry flight, with reserves of 30 and 45 minutes at the maximum endurance speed in cruise configuration. Its data item 4 asks for the segment-by-segment characteristics of each mission.

1. Compute the training flight and the ferry flight segment by segment with the performance module. Make one table per mission with the lecture's columns (slide 15): weight at the start, fuel, time, distance, altitude, speed and engine rating or lift coefficient.
2. Compute the RFP's prescribed segments as written: the training flight's climbs at 250 KCAS, its descents at idle thrust and its basic maneuvers at military thrust at Mach 0.60 and 10,000 ft, and the fixed-time allowances of both missions (taxi, takeoff, {term}`FCLP`, ferry approach) at the rating and configuration the RFP gives. For each segment whose speed, altitude or rating the RFP does not specify, state the value you chose and the reason.
3. Compute the reserves, 30 minutes for the training flight and 45 minutes for the ferry flight, at the maximum endurance speed in cruise configuration, at the weight at the start of the reserve and the altitude you state.
4. Check that the climb, cruise and descent of the ferry flight total 600 nm on internal fuel. Plot the fuel of the ferry flight against the cruise altitude and against the cruise speed. Mark your choice and the two speeds of Deliverable 1 step 4.
5. State the fuel each mission needs and the fuel the sizing code carries, and the difference. Name the mission that sizes the fuel.

## Deliverable 3: Climb, specific excess power and the flight envelope

The RFP requires a specific excess power of 90 ft/s at threshold and 125 ft/s at objective at 15,000 ft, MTOW less 50% fuel. Its data item 2 asks for the flight envelope, altitude against speed. Its training flight climbs to 20,000 ft at 250 KCAS.

1. Plot the rate of climb against speed at sea level and at 20,000 ft, at MTOW and the rating your mission climbs use, with the engine deck's thrust at each speed. Mark $V_Y$ and 250 KCAS.
2. Plot the specific excess power against Mach number at 15,000 ft, MTOW less 50% fuel, at 1 g. State the rating. Compare the largest value with 90 ft/s at threshold and 125 ft/s at objective.
3. Draw the flight envelope at 1 g (slide 33): pressure altitude against Mach number, with the stall boundary, the maximum operating speed and Mach number, and contours of specific excess power. State the weight and the rating. Report the service and absolute ceilings.
4. Mark every RFP condition and the cruise point of each mission on the envelope.

## Deliverable 4: Turns

The RFP requires a sustained load factor of +4.0 g at threshold and +6.0 g at objective at 15,000 ft, MTOW less 50% fuel, and names no speed for it.

1. Draw the turn-rate chart at 15,000 ft, MTOW less 50% fuel (slide 32): turn rate against Mach number, with the stall limit, the limit load factor of Deliverable 1, the maximum operating speed, the sustained line where the specific excess power is zero, and contours of specific excess power. State the rating.
2. Read the maximum sustained load factor and the Mach number where it occurs. Compare it with +4.0 g at threshold and +6.0 g at objective.
3. Compare the lift coefficient of the sustained turn with the break lift coefficient of the polar and with the maximum lift coefficient at that Mach number. If the sustained turn is beyond the break, state the drag you added.

## Deliverable 5: Field performance

The RFP limits the takeoff distance to 5,000 ft and the {term}`critical field length` to 6,000 ft at MTOW, and the landing distance to 5,000 ft at {term}`MLW`, all at 5,000 ft {term}`density altitude`, with the definitions of MIL-STD-3013B. Its data item 5 asks for the field lengths at MTOW and MLW.

1. Take the speed and distance definitions and the obstacle height from MIL-STD-3013B (slides 20 and 21; distances: High-Lift Systems deck, slide 2). State the rolling and braking friction coefficients with their source.
2. Compute the takeoff at MTOW and 5,000 ft density altitude (5,000 ft pressure altitude in the International Standard Atmosphere) by numerical integration of the ground run (slide 23), with the engine deck's thrust at that altitude, followed by the rotation, the transition and the climb to the obstacle. Check the ground run with the average acceleration. Compare the total with 5,000 ft.
3. Compute the critical field length at the same condition, by the MIL-STD-3013B definition. State how the definition applies to your number of engines. Compare it with 6,000 ft.
4. Compute the landing at MLW and 5,000 ft density altitude, from the obstacle to a full stop. State the speeds, the braking devices and the approach angle, and how the angle relates to the RFP's 3.5° FCLP glide slope. Compare the total with 5,000 ft. State how your MLW relates to the weight at the end of the FCLP segment.
5. Plot the takeoff, critical field and landing distances against weight. State which of the three requirements has the smallest margin.

## Deliverable 6: Maneuver V-n diagram

The RFP's data item 2 asks for a V-n diagram. This deliverable is the maneuver V-n diagram of the lecture, at the condition of the sustained load factor requirement. The training flight includes 30 minutes of basic maneuvers at military thrust at Mach 0.60 and 10,000 ft.

1. Draw the maneuver V-n diagram at 15,000 ft, MTOW less 50% fuel (slides 34 to 36): load factor against Mach number, with the 1 g stall speed, the stall line, the limit load factor of Deliverable 1, the maximum operating Mach number, the line where the specific excess power is zero, and contours of specific excess power. State the rating.
2. Mark the corner speed, +4.0 g and +6.0 g. Check that the line of zero specific excess power gives the sustained load factor of Deliverable 4.
3. Make the table of specific excess power by maneuver (slide 37) at the condition of the basic-maneuvers segment: Mach 0.60, 10,000 ft, military thrust, at the weight of that segment. Rows: level flight, the basic maneuvers you assume for the training flight with the load factor of each, the sustained turn, and the level turn, the pull-up and the pull-down at the limit load factor. Give the load factor, the turn rate, the specific excess power and the equivalent rate of climb of each. State which maneuvers hold speed and altitude and which do not.

## Deliverable 7: Compliance and sizing update

The AIAA rules list a compliance checklist among the report sections. The RFP asks for design or cost {term}`trade studies <trade study>` between its threshold and objective requirements. Its data item 1 asks for the trade study results that determine the minimum weight aircraft.

1. Make the compliance table: one row per RFP performance requirement and one for each of data items 2 to 5, with the condition, the requirement at threshold and at objective, your value and the margin.
2. For each requirement not met at threshold, state the change that would meet it, run the sizing code with that change and report the new takeoff weight. If the change moves the design point outside the A06 {term}`feasible region`, state it and stop there.
3. For each requirement met at threshold and not at objective, state the change that would meet the objective and the takeoff weight it costs.
4. For each margin larger than the uncertainty of its result, state whether the aircraft carries weight it does not need, and which input you would change.
5. Enter the results in ASORT and run the sizing code again.

## Deliverable 8: Defense and reflection

Present the design defense on two slides:

1. The requirement with the smallest margin, the input that sets it, and the change in margin when that input changes.
2. The mission that sizes the fuel, with the margin of the other mission.
3. The source of the limit load factor and the maximum dive speed.
4. The result with the largest uncertainty and the data that would reduce it.

Any member may be asked to present any slide.

Each member writes a reflection of at most 150 words. Name the result you contributed most to and how you checked it. Name the result you would check first if the A07 weights changed. Add one sentence naming the AI tools you used and for what.
