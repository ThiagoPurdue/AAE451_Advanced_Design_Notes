## Objective

The {term}`RFP` states what the customer needs and the limits your aircraft must meet. It is written for a customer, not a designer, and it mixes the two in the same prose. This assignment separates them and records both as something you can design against. The concept of operations sits between them: it fixes the missions and operating conditions the requirements are then written against.

You will:

1. Deconstruct the RFP into classified, sourced statements.
2. Write the stakeholder needs.
3. Describe how the aircraft will be operated, as a concept of operations.
4. Write the system requirements that address those needs.
5. Trace the needs and requirements together.

These requirements are the reference for the rest of the semester. Later assignments design to them, and at PDR your performance is measured against them. A requirement that cannot be measured cannot be shown to be met.

Your team designs to the AIAA 2026-27 Undergraduate Team Aircraft Design RFP, the Navy Jet Trainer, summarized in [](../navy-rfp/navy-rfp.md). State it on your title slide. The RFP uses threshold and objective values. All deliverables are required.

## Deliverable 1: Team charter

One slide, plus a one-page charter in the appendix.

- Every member named, with a photograph.
- Each member's role for the semester, and who deputizes for them.
- Meeting cadence, and where minutes are kept.
- Your decision rule for when the team does not agree.
- Your procedure for a member who is not delivering, and when it reaches the instructor.

## Deliverable 2: RFP deconstruction

One table, one row per statement extracted from the RFP. Every row carries its source.

Classify each statement:

- **Constraint.** A limit that bounds the design space and is not traded.
- **Capability.** Something the aircraft must be able to do.
- **Scoring driver.** A basis on which the proposal is judged, with no pass mark.
- **Assumption.** Something you decided because the RFP does not state it.

Mark {term}`threshold` and {term}`objective` values separately. A design that meets every threshold is acceptable. The gap between the two is where later trade studies live.

:::{warning}
A statement in the RFP that appears in no row is something your design will not be obliged to do. A row that traces to nothing in the RFP is yours, and belongs in the assumption class.
:::

## Deliverable 3: Stakeholder needs

Build a set of needs taken from the RFP, in the Requirements Editor.

- State each {term}`need` **independently of the solution**: what the customer wants to be able to do, not what your aircraft will be. A need that implies a configuration, a powerplant or a structural concept is a design decision that has escaped into the needs.
- Phrase needs as needs, "the customer needs to be able to...", not as "shall" statements.
- **Group the needs by category.** Work the categories out from your RFP.
- **Record the source of each need** as a custom attribute: where in the RFP it came from. Mark the needs your team added. These are the assumptions from Deliverable 2.
- Save as `TeamXX_A01_Needs.slreqx`.

Expect fewer needs than requirements. One need usually takes several requirements to pin down, which is why the two are kept in separate sets.

## Deliverable 4: Concept of operations

Describe how the aircraft is operated, from the operator's side. One to three slides, plus the exported document `TeamXX_A01_CONOPS.pdf`.

Cover:

- **Operating stakeholders.** Who handles the aircraft: aircrew, ground crew, maintainers, the operating authority and the student, and what each does with it.
- **Operational environment.** Where it is based and operated, the field and airspace it uses, and the atmospheric and surface conditions it works in. Take these from your RFP and mark anything you assumed.
- **Missions.** The distinct missions your RFP calls for, each as an ordered sequence of phases from pre-flight to recovery, with the transition between each phase named. State a segment condition only where the RFP gives it.
- **Mission profile.** For each mission, an altitude-against-range or altitude-against-time diagram with every segment labeled. Put a number on a segment only where the RFP supplies it.
- **Reference scenarios.** One nominal end-to-end scenario, and at least one off-nominal: a diversion, a weather hold, a degraded system or a single failure, each carried through to how the operator recovers.
- **Operational constraints that drive the design.** Turnaround time, crew workload, maintenance access, ground handling, basing and transport.

Trace each mission and scenario to the stakeholder need it serves. A need that surfaces only here is one your needs set is missing. Add it to `TeamXX_A01_Needs.slreqx` so it carries into Deliverables 5 and 6.

:::{warning}
State what the aircraft must operate through, not how it will be built to do so. A concept of operations that names a configuration, a powerplant or a system architecture has made a design decision that belongs later.
:::

## Deliverable 5: System requirements

Create a second set holding the requirements that address those needs. Use the form **"The system shall..."**.

A good {term}`requirement` is **SMART**:

- **Specific.** One obligation per statement, stated unambiguously, with the condition under which it applies. "Take off within a distance *and* climb to an altitude" is two requirements and can be half satisfied. State what must be true, not how you will achieve it.
- **Measurable.** A quantity with units, or a condition plainly met or not met. "The aircraft shall have good handling qualities" settles nothing either way.
- **Achievable.** Possible within the RFP limits. A requirement no design could meet is an error in the requirement, not a stretch goal.
- **Relevant.** Addresses a stakeholder need. One addressing none is either unnecessary or evidence that a need is missing.
- **Traceable.** Carries its source and rationale, and leads forward to the evidence that will show it has been met.

SMART is also used for goal setting, where T is time-bound. For requirements it is traceability.

For each requirement:

- **Fill in both the summary and the description.** The summary is the short handle used in later views. The description is the requirement itself, with units.
- **Record which need it addresses.**
- **Add a custom attribute `Rationale`** giving why it is necessary and what part of the RFP it addresses. Write "From the RFP" where it is taken directly.
- **Add a custom attribute `Threshold/Objective`** marking which value the requirement states. This is the RFP's own structure and every later trade study turns on it, so it has to survive into the requirement rather than stopping at the deconstruction table.
- **Add a custom attribute `Verification Method`**: analysis, inspection, demonstration or test. Name the method now. Which assignment supplies the evidence is settled later.
- **Nest requirements under a parent** where a group shares a subject.
- Save as `TeamXX_A01_Requirements.slreqx`.

:::{tip} How many?
Expect roughly 20 to 40 needs and 50 to 90 requirements. A set far outside that range usually means the two have been conflated: a handful of needs restated as requirements, or every RFP sentence copied in verbatim as its own obligation. The count is guidance, not a target. Coverage is what is graded.
:::

Write the rationale as you go rather than afterwards. Later in the semester a requirement will block a design you want, and the question will be whether it came from the RFP and is fixed, or from your team and can be revisited.

## Deliverable 6: Traceability matrix

Generate a matrix with stakeholder needs on one axis and system requirements on the other, and export it as a PDF. Save as `TeamXX_A01_Traceability.pdf`.

Read it in both directions:

- An **orphan** need, one that no requirement addresses, is something the customer asked for that your aircraft is not obliged to do.
- **Gold plating**, a requirement addressing no need, is a self-imposed constraint that will cost weight and performance. Justify it or drop it.

Both are cheap to fix this week and expensive to fix at PDR.

Every mission-driven need from your concept of operations belongs on this matrix. An operational need with no requirement is an orphan like any other.

## How this is graded

Out of 100 points.

:::{table} Grading criteria.
:label: tbl-a01-grading

| Criterion | Points | What earns full marks |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 1. RFP deconstruction | 20 | Every RFP statement extracted, classified, sourced, with threshold and objective separated |
| 2. Stakeholder needs | 15 | Solution-independent, grouped by a category scheme derived from the RFP, every entry sourced or marked as your assumption |
| 3. Concept of operations | 15 | Operating stakeholders and environment stated, every RFP mission broken into phases, one nominal and one off-nominal scenario, each mission and scenario traced to a need |
| 4. System requirements | 25 | SMART throughout, all four attributes populated, coverage of the needs complete |
| 5. Traceability | 15 | No orphan needs and no gold plating, or a written justification for each one kept |
| 6. Charter and presentation | 10 | Charter complete; deck legible, organized and defensible under questioning |

:::

Criteria 1 to 4 carry three quarters of the marks. None of them reward volume. A short, well-formed set beats a long one that restates the RFP.

## Before you submit

- Title slide names your team, its members, and the RFP you are designing to.
- Charter: roles, deputies, cadence, decision rule, non-delivery procedure.
- Deconstruction table: every row classified and sourced, threshold and objective separated.
- `TeamXX_A01_Needs.slreqx`: grouped by category, source on every entry, none phrased as "shall", none naming a solution.
- `TeamXX_A01_CONOPS.pdf`: operating stakeholders and environment stated, every RFP mission broken into phases, one nominal and one off-nominal scenario, each mission and scenario traced to a need, no solution named.
- `TeamXX_A01_Requirements.slreqx`: "shall" form, SMART, `Rationale`, `Threshold/Objective` and `Verification Method` on every requirement, and the need each addresses recorded.
- `TeamXX_A01_Traceability.pdf`: no orphan needs and no gold plating, or a written justification for each one kept.
- Every figure readable at presentation size, and no series distinguished by color alone.
