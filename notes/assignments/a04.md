## Objective

This assignment defines the lifting system of your aircraft: the airfoil, the wing geometry, and the high-lift devices, realized as the first dimensioned layout and surface loft. The SRR action items are answered here. The drag model and the aerodynamic deck follow in the next assignment, after the drag lectures. The work uses AI tools under the rules below.

You will:

1. Select the airfoil against criteria traced to your requirements.
2. Define the wing geometry.
3. Size the high-lift system and estimate maximum lift.
4. Update the layout and produce the surface loft.
5. Work with a model at each step, and record what its input changed in your design.
6. Answer the SRR action items and defend the results.

Your team keeps the Navy Jet Trainer {term}`RFP` from A01. All deliverables are required. The design point is not fixed in this assignment: carry {term}`wing loading` as the {term}`ASORT` value your sizing uses, and treat every quantity that depends on it as provisional until the constraint-diagram assignment fixes the design point.

:::{seealso}
The methods are in Lecture 5: the airfoil in [](../lecture-05/airfoil.md), the planform and the lift model in [](../lecture-05/wing-geometry-and-lift.md). The high-lift devices are in the high-lift lecture, on Brightspace.
:::

## How AI is used in this assignment

1. Draft each deliverable yourselves. Date the draft before a model sees it. A deliverable drafted by a model receives no credit.
2. Deliverables 1 through 4 each end with a working session. Give the model the dated draft, your RFP, your requirements, and your data. Ask it to critique the draft and to extend the work as the deliverable states.
3. Run each session when its draft is ready, not at the end of the assignment.
4. Before adopting a suggestion: restate it in your own words, state why it applies to your mission and requirements, and cite its source. A suggestion without a source is recorded as a team assumption.
5. The team selects the airfoil, the wing geometry, and the high-lift system, and defends each in its own words. "The AI said so" is not a justification.
6. Put every prompt and every full model return in the deck appendix.
7. Concept and tool questions are allowed at any time, such as what a form factor represents or how a drag bucket is read.

## Deliverable 1: Airfoil selection

1. Write the selection criteria and trace each to a requirement or a mission segment: the design lift coefficient your cruise implies, maximum lift, the thickness the structure and the fuel volume need, and the Reynolds and Mach range your missions cover.
2. Assemble at least three candidate airfoils with published data. Cite the source of every curve; do not use coordinates or polars a model produced. The candidates must be credible at the RFP's maximum operating Mach number.
3. Compare the candidates on the criteria. Select one and record the runner-up.
4. Plot the selected airfoil's lift curve and drag polar from the published data, with the design point marked. Date the draft.
5. Working session. Give the model the dated criteria table and your candidate list. Ask it to critique the criteria against your requirements and to name candidate airfoils you did not consider. A suggested airfoil enters the comparison only with published data your team locates and cites.

## Deliverable 2: Wing geometry

1. Compute the wing area from the ASORT wing loading and the converged takeoff weight.
2. Choose aspect ratio, taper, sweep, and twist. Justify each with the method from the wing-geometry lecture or a citable source, against your requirements. Relate the sweep and thickness choice to the RFP's maximum operating Mach number.
3. State the span, root and tip chords, and mean aerodynamic chord that follow. Enter every value in ASORT. Date the geometry.
4. Estimate the wing's lift-curve slope and the maximum lift coefficient of the clean wing from the airfoil data and the planform, with the method cited.
5. Working session. Give the model the dated geometry and the requirement set. Ask it to critique the choices against the requirements and to name the trade you have not examined. Test any you had not considered and report what moved.

## Deliverable 3: High lift

1. Choose the high-lift devices from the types in the high-lift lecture. Justify the choice against the field-performance requirement the RFP states.
2. Estimate the maximum lift increment of the chosen devices and the resulting takeoff and landing maximum lift coefficients, with the method and source cited.
3. State the stall speeds the estimates imply at the ASORT wing loading, and check them against every speed-linked requirement in your set.
4. Working session. Give the model the dated high-lift estimate. Ask it to critique the increment against published data for the device type and to flag any speed-linked requirement you did not check.

## Deliverable 4: Layout and loft

1. Draw a dimensioned three-view of the aircraft with the Deliverable 2 wing and the Deliverable 3 high-lift devices. Include principal dimensions and control-surface outlines. Export as `TeamXX_A04_Layout.pdf`.
2. Arrange the interior: the cockpit to the dimensions the RFP states, fuel volume, landing gear, and equipment. State the fuel volume check explicitly: required volume against available volume, using the wing the airfoil thickness now defines.
3. State the CG range the arrangement implies and whether the wing position accommodates it. A single CG point is acceptable only with the assumption stated.
4. Loft the outer surface on the 3DEXPERIENCE platform, with the selected airfoil sections. The loft matches the layout dimensions. Export as `TeamXX_A04_Loft.zip`.
5. Enter every layout dimension in ASORT. The layout, the loft, and the code read the same values. Date the drawing.
6. Working session. Give the model the dated layout dimensions, the ASORT file, and the RFP's stated dimensions. Ask it to flag every inconsistency among the three, and to name what the layout makes difficult that the aerodynamic model did not capture. Check each flag before changing the layout.

## Deliverable 5: AI use record

Two slides.

1. List the working sessions: the deliverable, the tool, the date, and who ran it.
2. List what you adopted or adapted, each restated in your own words, with its source or its team-assumption label, and what it changed in the deliverable.
3. List what you rejected, with the engineering reason.
4. Show the exchange that most improved your result: what you asked, what the model returned, your judgment, and the change.

A session that changed nothing is a valid result. State why it changed nothing.

The record is graded against the prompts and returns in the appendix. A session with no documented model return receives no credit.

## Deliverable 6: Action items, defense, disclosure and reflection

**SRR action items.** One slide. Every action item from your {term}`SRR`, with its owner and the response. An item answered by analysis points at the deliverable that carries the analysis. Record any change to the baselined requirement sets as a written {term}`waiver` in the deck appendix, and execute the plan your SRR defense stated for the most-at-risk requirement; report its status.

**Design defense.** Two slides. This material carries forward to the aerodynamic-deck assignment and the {term}`PDR` narrative.

1. State the criterion that, changed, would flip the airfoil selection to the runner-up. State why your criteria are right.
2. State the wing-geometry choice you would revisit first if the constraint diagram moves the wing loading, and why.
3. State which requirement the lift results put most at risk, and what the drag model or the constraint diagram must resolve.

Any member presents any slide on request. Text that mirrors model phrasing scores as Developing, regardless of content.

**Individual reflection.** Each member submits 150 to 250 words answering:

1. Name the exchange that most improved your aerodynamic model. State what made the model's input specific to your aircraft rather than generic.
2. Name one decision the team made without the model. State why it had to be yours.
3. What will you change about how you use a model in the next assignment?

Add one paragraph disclosing the tools used, their purpose, and what was not AI-generated.
