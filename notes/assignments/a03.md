## Objective

This assignment refines the A02 takeoff-weight estimate, generates and selects the aircraft {term}`configuration`, and analyzes the design missions in detail. The work uses AI tools under the rules below and is graded with the course AI-Assisted Homework Rubric.

You will:

1. Refine the sizing and close the open A02 certification checks.
2. Generate candidate configurations that satisfy your requirements.
3. Select one configuration with a documented decision method.
4. Analyze the design missions and choose the values the RFP leaves open.
5. Work with a model at each step, and record what its input changed in your design.
6. Defend the selection and carry its open risks into A04.

Your team keeps the Navy Jet Trainer {term}`RFP` from A01. All deliverables are required.

Deliverables 1 through 4 are part of your {term}`SRR`: they form the initial-sizing and configuration down-select content of the SRR baseline document, and your SRR presentation draws on them. Requirements are baselined at the close of the SRR. From that point, record any change to the requirement sets as a written {term}`waiver` in the deck appendix.

The methods are in the lectures: the arrangement options in [](../lecture-03/configuration.md), the morphological chart and the Pugh matrix in [](../lecture-04/concept-selection.md), and the sizing loop in [](../lecture-02/mission-fuel.md). The two prescribed missions are in [](../navy-rfp/navy-rfp.md).

## How AI is used in this assignment

1. Draft each deliverable yourselves. Date the draft before a model sees it. A deliverable drafted by a model receives no credit.
2. Deliverables 1 through 4 each end with a working session. Give the model the dated draft, your RFP, your requirements, and your data. Ask it to critique the draft and to extend the work as the deliverable states.
3. Run each session when its draft is ready, not at the end of the assignment.
4. Before adopting a suggestion: restate it in your own words, state why it applies to your mission and requirements, and cite its source. A suggestion without a source is recorded as a team assumption.
5. The team selects the configuration, sets the criterion weights, and defends both in its own words. "The AI said so" is not a justification.
6. Put every prompt and every full model return in the deck appendix.
7. Concept and tool questions are allowed at any time, such as what a Pugh matrix datum is or what the endurance equation assumes.

## Deliverable 1: Sizing refinement

1. Replace the historical climb fraction with a computed estimate, using the climb method from Raymer chapter 6. Recompute every segment your {term}`CONOPS` states as a distance or a time.
2. Restructure the code around one authoritative source of truth ({term}`ASORT`): a single parameter file that every script reads. Do not duplicate constants in code.
3. Re-run the certification checks carried from A02. Move each one to pass, fail, or still open, with the reason.
4. Report the new converged takeoff weight next to the A02 value. State what changed and why. Date the draft.
5. Update the sensitivity of takeoff weight to the empty-weight adjustment.
6. Working session. Give the model the dated ASORT file and the sizing code. Ask it to critique the climb method against Raymer chapter 6, to flag constants duplicated outside ASORT, and to flag segments inconsistent with your CONOPS. Adopt under the rules above.

## Deliverable 2: Configuration generation

1. Build a {term}`morphological matrix <morphological chart>`: one row per major arrangement choice, one column per option. Choose the rows yourselves. Date the matrix.
2. Compose at least three distinct configurations from the matrix. Distinct means different arrangement decisions, not resized copies of one concept.
3. Check each configuration against your CONOPS and requirement set. Record every disqualifier with the requirement or RFP statement it violates.
4. Sketch each surviving configuration as one labeled three-view. Hand sketches are acceptable.
5. Working session. Give the model the dated matrix, your candidates, and your requirement set. Ask it for rows, options, and candidate configurations you did not compose. A suggestion enters the matrix only after you check it against your requirements and CONOPS. A candidate that enters is scored in Deliverable 3.

## Deliverable 3: Configuration selection

1. Derive the selection criteria from your requirements and CONOPS. State each criterion and the requirement it traces to.
2. Build a {term}`Pugh matrix` with a named {term}`datum`. Score every candidate. Date the matrix.
3. Re-score once with the leading candidate as the new datum. State whether the leader holds.
4. Record the selected configuration and the runner-up.
5. If the selection forces a requirement change, record it as a waiver in the deck appendix.
6. Working session. Give the model the dated Pugh matrix and the criteria. Ask it to critique the scoring, to name criteria your requirements imply that you did not use, and to find the weight changes that would change the winner. If the session changes a score, a criterion, or the candidate list, re-run the selection and state whether the leader holds. The weights stay the team's.

## Deliverable 4: Mission analysis

1. Build one segment table per {term}`design mission` in the sizing code: altitude, speed, distance or time, configuration, and energy source for every segment. Model both missions the RFP prescribes.
2. Resolve every choice the RFP leaves open with a documented decision: unspecified segment speeds, cruise altitude, the reserve interpretation. Enter each in ASORT, dated, with a source or a team-assumption label.
3. Sweep the free variables, cruise altitude and speed at minimum. Present the sweep as a figure with the chosen point marked. State why the optimum sits where it does and which constraint binds first.
4. Report which mission sizes the fuel, with the segment-by-segment breakdown of each mission.
5. Present one slide per mission: the profile diagram, the segment table with sources, and the sweep figure. Date the tables.
6. Working session. Give the model the dated mission tables and the sweep. Ask it to critique the segment assumptions against your CONOPS and RFP, and to name sensitivities you did not test. Test the named ones you had not considered and report them with the Deliverable 1 sensitivity.

## Deliverable 5: AI use record

Two slides.

1. List the working sessions: the deliverable, the tool, the date, and who ran it.
2. List what you adopted or adapted, each restated in your own words, with its source or its team-assumption label, and what it changed in the deliverable.
3. List what you rejected, with the engineering reason.
4. Show the exchange that most improved your result: what you asked, what the model returned, your judgment, and the change.
5. State whether the configuration selection changed after any re-run.

A session that changed nothing is a valid result. State why it changed nothing.

The record is graded against the prompts and returns in the appendix. A session with no documented model return receives no credit.

## Deliverable 6: Design defense, disclosure and reflection

**Design defense.** Two slides. This material carries into A04 and the {term}`PDR` narrative.

1. State the single criterion-weight change that would flip the Pugh selection to the runner-up. State why your weighting is right.
2. State which mission sizes the fuel, and the margin to the other mission. State the mission choice your takeoff weight is most sensitive to.
3. State which certification check or requirement the selected configuration puts most at risk, and the A04 plan for it.

Any member presents any slide on request. Text that mirrors model phrasing scores as Developing, regardless of content.

**Individual reflection.** Each member submits 150 to 250 words answering:

1. Name the exchange that most changed your configuration work. State what made the model's input specific to your aircraft rather than generic.
2. Name one decision the team made without the model. State why it had to be yours.
3. What will you change about how you use a model in A04?

Add one paragraph disclosing the tools used, their purpose, and what was not AI-generated.
