## Objective

This assignment selects the propulsion system and builds the installed engine deck. The constraint diagram and the performance analysis use that deck.

Three terms are used throughout:

- {term}`Engine deck`: thrust and fuel flow against altitude, speed, ambient temperature, and rating.
- {term}`Rating`: a power setting the maker names, such as idle, maximum continuous, or takeoff.
- {term}`Performance condition`: a point requirement the {term}`RFP` states at a weight, altitude, and day, as opposed to a mission segment.

You will:

1. Derive the propulsion requirements from the mission and compare engine classes.
2. Select the engines from cited published data.
3. Build the installed engine deck and implement it in the sizing code.
4. Integrate the propulsion system in the layout.
5. Defend the selection.

All deliverables are required. {term}`Wing loading` and thrust-to-weight stay provisional until the constraint-diagram assignment fixes the design point.

**AI tools.** Draft the work yourselves. An AI tool may check or extend a draft. Restate anything you adopt in your own words, with its source; put every prompt and full return in the deck appendix. Do not use engine values a model produced. The team makes and defends the selection.

## Deliverable 1: Propulsion requirements and engine class

1. State the drag polar you use for each configuration your segments fly, with its source.
2. Build one table per design mission from your A03 mission analysis. One row per segment: altitude, speed, configuration, time or distance, the rating the RFP names or your mission assumes, and the thrust required from the drag polar at the segment weight.
3. Add the performance conditions the RFP states, at threshold and at objective, with the weight, altitude, and day the RFP attaches to each. Where the RFP states none, state your assumption. Record in {term}`ASORT` every choice the RFP leaves open. The RFP refers to MIL-STD-3013B for the definitions.
4. Place the requirements on the engine-class map from the lecture. Compare at least two classes with the engine-class table from the lecture. The RFP specifies a jet; compare jet engine classes.
5. Define at least two propulsion trade-offs you will carry out in this assignment. For each, state the quantities traded, the figure of merit, the requirement it serves, and the deliverable where it is run.

## Deliverable 2: Candidates and selection

1. Assemble at least three candidates from published sources: textbook propulsion data and maker's data.
2. Record for each candidate, with a source: sea-level static thrust, SFC, dry weight, length and diameter, airflow and throttle ratio where published, and bypass ratio.
3. Candidates may differ in the number of engines. State the criteria you use for the number, with sources, and the decision.
4. Name the segment or performance condition that sizes the engine. Compare the candidates at that segment or condition: thrust available, fuel flow, and weight. Use the lecture relations for this comparison; Deliverable 3 replaces them. Select one and record the second-ranked candidate. State type, manufacturer, and model, as the RFP requires.
5. A scaled engine is allowed. Scale from a cited reference engine with the rubber-engine relations from the lecture, and state it as a derivative of that engine.

## Deliverable 3: Installed engine deck and mission fuel

1. Build the deck over every altitude, speed, temperature, and rating in your Deliverable 1 tables. Use the maker's deck where one is published. Otherwise build one from the lapse and fuel-flow relations in the lecture, matched to the published sea-level static values; for part power, scale a published part-power curve for an engine of the same class. Plot the relations against the published points on one figure. State every relation used beyond the published data, with its source. A published deck is not a selection criterion.
2. Apply installation effects with values and sources: inlet recovery, nozzle, bleed, power extraction, and the power and bleed drawn by the aircraft systems, with the systems named.
3. State the propulsion group weight line by line, with a source per line, in the breakdown the RFP's weight statement uses. Enter it in ASORT.
4. Plot each Deliverable 1 segment and performance condition on the installed deck. A segment flown at a rating the RFP names is plotted at that rating. Read the fuel flow at each point. Show the margin to maximum thrust at every performance condition, at threshold and at objective. Where the RFP states a density altitude, convert it to the pressure altitude and temperature the deck uses and state the conversion.
5. Code the deck as a function that reads ASORT: inputs altitude, speed, temperature, and rating; outputs thrust available and fuel flow. ASORT holds the deck file path and the installation values. Replace the fixed SFC values from A03 with values read from the deck. Keep the A04 geometry fixed and re-converge the takeoff weight. Report the change in mission fuel and in takeoff weight against the A04 code, and whether the A04 fuel volume check is still met.

## Deliverable 4: Integration

The inlet:

1. State the inlet design point and the reason for it. Compute the capture area there from the engine airflow and the secondary airflow; state how you obtain the airflow. Check that it passes the airflow at takeoff and at the maximum-Mach condition. Compare it with the inlet of an aircraft that uses your engine or its reference engine.
2. Choose the inlet type and location. State the pressure recovery you enter in the deck, with its source.
3. Size the duct length and check the included angle against the lecture limit. If the inlet is mounted on the fuselage, size the boundary-layer diverter with a cited method.
4. Add the inlet, duct, and engine to the layout and update the CG.

Enter every value in ASORT.

## Deliverable 5: Defense and reflection

**Design defense.** Two slides. The constraint-diagram assignment uses them.

1. Name the segment or condition that sized the propulsion system, and the margin to the next most demanding one.
2. State which objective values the selection meets, and the engine change needed for each objective value not met.
3. State the change in requirements or data that would make the second-ranked candidate the selection.
4. State the requirement least likely to be met with the selected propulsion system, and what the constraint diagram must resolve. Report the status of the requirement A04 identified as least likely to be met.

Any member presents any slide on request.

**Individual reflection.** Each member submits at most 150 words: the propulsion decision you contributed most to and its justification, and the result you would check first if the constraint diagram changes the design point. Add one sentence naming the AI tools you used and what they were used for.

## Before you submit

- Every segment and performance condition has its thrust required at a stated weight, altitude, and day.
- Every candidate value is cited in `TeamXX_A05_EngineData.xlsx`; the selection and the second-ranked candidate are recorded at the named sizing condition; the Deliverable 1 trade-offs are run and reported.
- The installed deck covers every Deliverable 1 condition, is checked against published points, and every relation used beyond them is stated.
- The sizing code reads the deck through ASORT, runs on another member's machine, and the change against A04 is reported.
- The propulsion system is sized, in ASORT and in `TeamXX_A05_Layout.pdf`, with the CG updated.
- The prompts and the models' full returns are in the deck appendix.
