## Objective

Select the {term}`design point` of your aircraft: the {term}`wing loading` $W_{TO}/S$ and the {term}`thrust-to-weight ratio` $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$. Earlier assignments used provisional values. The performance assignment starts from this design point.

You will:

1. Define every boundary and its inputs.
2. Code the constraint equations and check the code against two published examples.
3. Draw the {term}`constraint diagram`.
4. Choose the design point and re-run the sizing code until it converges.
5. Defend the design point.

All deliverables are required. Use the International Standard Atmosphere, as your {term}`RFP` requires.

**AI tools.** Do the work yourselves first. An AI tool may then check or extend a draft. Restate what you adopt in your own words, with its source, and put every prompt and response in the deck appendix. Do not use drag, lift or engine values from an AI tool.

## Deliverable 1: Boundaries and inputs

1. Make a table in the deck with one row per boundary: every RFP performance requirement (maximum operating Mach, {term}`specific excess power`, sustained load factor, takeoff distance, {term}`critical field length`, landing distance), at {term}`threshold` and at {term}`objective` where both are given, plus every boundary your requirement set, your design missions or MIL-HDBK-516C adds, with its source.
2. Each row records: the requirement ID and the lecture case; $\beta$ and the RFP weight; altitude, temperature and Mach number; load factor and specific excess power; configuration, $C_{D0}$, $K_1$, $K_2$, $C_{DR}$ and the compressibility drag increment; engine {term}`rating` and $\alpha$; for takeoff and landing rows, $C_{L,max}$ and the speed ratio. Every value comes from {term}`ASORT` or carries a citation.
3. State the rating used at each condition, with its MIL-STD-3013B definition.
4. The RFP gives no speed for the specific excess power and sustained load factor requirements. For each, sweep Mach at 15,000 ft and plot the required $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$, one line per wing loading. State whether you read the requirement at one speed or over a band, with support from MIL-STD-3013B. Record the choice in ASORT.
5. For the sustained turn, compare the lift coefficient required with the usable maximum at that Mach number. Where lift limits the turn, draw that limit as a boundary.
6. Takeoff and landing rows: use the A05 conversion of the 5,000 ft {term}`density altitude`; use the MIL-STD-3013B definitions of the three distances and state the segments each includes; state $k_{TO}$, $k_{TD}$, the braking friction coefficient and the maximum landing weight, with sources.
7. State the critical field length method for your number of engines, with its source.

## Deliverable 2: Constraint code and check

1. Write a MATLAB function that reads ASORT, takes one Deliverable 1 row and a vector of $W_{TO}/S$, and returns the required $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$ or, for a row that does not depend on thrust, the limiting $W_{TO}/S$. Takeoff and landing rows use the ground-roll and braking relations from the lecture or a cited method.
2. Reproduce the Cessna 162 (Gudmundsson Example 3-1) and the T-45C (lecture appendix). Show your values next to the lecture's and explain every difference.
3. Add the function to the sizing code. Both read the same ASORT.

## Deliverable 3: Constraint diagram

1. Plot every boundary on one diagram: $W_{TO}/S$ in lb/ft² against $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$. Threshold and objective of one requirement in one color with different line styles. Label each boundary with its requirement ID.
2. Shade the {term}`feasible region` at threshold. Mark the feasible region at objective.
3. Plot the A02 database aircraft from their published takeoff weight, reference wing area and sea-level static thrust. State whether each thrust is installed. State what their positions say about your inputs.
4. Plot the provisional design point of A03 to A05.
5. Name the boundaries that form the edge of the feasible region, at threshold and at objective.

## Deliverable 4: Design point and sizing closure

1. Choose $W_{TO}/S$ and $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$. State the {term}`active boundaries <active boundary>`, the margin to each and the reasons for the choice.
2. For each requirement, state whether the design point meets the threshold or the objective value, and why. A mix is allowed.
3. State whether the engine is the A05 engine at its published thrust or a scaled engine. Published: show that the available $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$ at the converged takeoff weight exceeds the required value; if not, state what you changed. Scaled: state the reference engine, the scale factor and the resulting weight and dimensions, with the propulsion lecture's relations.
4. Enter the design point in ASORT and re-run the sizing code. Update $\beta$, the A04 geometry and the A05 deck on each pass. Stop when the design point and the takeoff weight no longer change. State the tolerance and the number of iterations.
5. Report the change from A05 in takeoff weight, empty weight, fuel weight, reference wing area, span and sea-level static thrust. State whether the A04 fuel volume check, the stall-speed checks and the CG range still hold.
6. Threshold and objective trade, as your RFP requires: the design point for each set, with its takeoff weight, reference wing area and thrust from the sizing code. One re-run is enough for the point you do not select.
7. Sensitivity: change one input at a time ($C_{D0}$, $K_1$, the compressibility drag increment, $C_{L,max}$, $\alpha$, $\beta$). State each change and its basis. Report which boundaries move, by how much at the design $W_{TO}/S$, and whether the design point stays feasible. Name every input whose uncertainty exceeds the design point's margin to the boundary it moves.

## Deliverable 5: Defense and reflection

**Design defense.** Two slides.

1. The design point, the active boundaries and the margin to each.
2. One active boundary with its requirement changed: how far the boundary moves and how much the takeoff weight changes. State whether the requirement comes from the RFP or from your team.
3. The objective values met. For each one not met, the change in $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$ or $W_{TO}/S$ that would meet it and the change in takeoff weight.
4. The input with the largest effect on the design point and the data that would reduce its uncertainty. The status of the requirement A05 named least likely to be met, and the one now least likely to be met.

Any member may be asked to present any slide.

**Individual reflection.** At most 150 words: the boundary or input you contributed most to and how you justified it; the result you would check first if the drag polar or the engine deck changed; one sentence naming the AI tools you used and for what.
