## Objective

Run the sizing code over a matrix of {term}`wing loading` $W_{TO}/S$ and {term}`thrust-to-weight ratio` $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$, draw the {term}`carpet plot`, and read the lightest aircraft that meets every {term}`RFP` performance requirement. Select the {term}`design point` for {term}`PDR`. The inputs are the A05 installed {term}`engine deck`, the A06 {term}`constraint diagram` and design point, the A07 weights and trimmed cruise polar, the A04 takeoff and landing polars, and the A08 performance module and compliance table, all from {term}`ASORT`. Use the models of the sizing code. Do not build a second model for this assignment.

You will:

1. Define the {term}`trade study` and run the {term}`sizing matrix`.
2. Draw the {term}`sizing plot` and the {term}`cross-plots <cross-plot>`.
3. Draw the carpet plot and find the {term}`lightest feasible point`.
4. Repeat the study at three values of a third design variable.
5. Compute the {term}`sensitivities <sensitivity>` of the takeoff weight.
6. Select the design point for PDR.
7. Defend the results.

All deliverables are required. Every input comes from ASORT or carries a citation. State the weight, altitude, speed and engine {term}`rating` of every constraint value. Use the International Standard Atmosphere, as the RFP requires.

**AI tools.** Do the work yourselves first. An AI tool may then check or extend a draft. Restate what you adopt in your own words, with its source, and put every prompt and response in the deck appendix. Do not use takeoff weights, constraint values or sensitivities from an AI tool.

## Deliverable 1: Study definition and sizing matrix

The RFP's performance data item 1 asks for "Trade study results to determine the minimum weight aircraft." The RFP requires a maximum operating speed of Mach 0.78 at {term}`threshold` and 0.90 at {term}`objective` at 25,000 ft pressure altitude, {term}`MTOW` less 50% fuel. It requires a {term}`specific excess power` of 90 ft/s at threshold and 125 ft/s at objective, and a sustained {term}`load factor` of +4.0 g at threshold and +6.0 g at objective, both at 15,000 ft pressure altitude, MTOW less 50% fuel. It limits the takeoff distance to 5,000 ft and the {term}`critical field length` to 6,000 ft at MTOW, and the landing distance to 5,000 ft at {term}`MLW`, all at 5,000 ft {term}`density altitude`.

1. Take the takeoff weight $W_{TO}$ as the {term}`measure of merit`, from data item 1 (slide 3). The two variables are $W_{TO}/S$ and $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$. List every input the study holds fixed, in two groups: the quantities your team selects, and the inputs it does not select. Deliverable 4 chooses its third variable from the first group.
2. Make a table with one row per constraint (slide 11): each RFP performance requirement on the A06 constraint diagram, and each other A06 boundary that the A08 performance module computes. Each row gives the {term}`limit` at threshold, the limit at objective where the RFP gives one, and the weight, altitude, speed and engine rating recorded in A06. In every run, compute the weight of each condition from that run's takeoff weight.
3. Scale the A05 engine to the sea-level static thrust each run needs. If A06 selected an engine without scaling it, add one constraint to the table of step 2: the thrust the run needs, with the thrust of the selected engine as its limit.
4. Write a matrix script for the sizing code that sets $W_{TO}/S$ and $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$ on a copy of ASORT, runs the sizing code to convergence, and returns $W_{TO}$ and the value of every constraint of step 2 from the A08 performance module. Deliverables 3 to 6 use it.
5. Center the matrix on the design point in ASORT after A08 (slide 4). Take three values of each variable. State the spacing and the reason for it.
6. Make the nine runs, each to convergence. State the tolerance. Tabulate $W_{TO}$ and every constraint value of each run, in bold where a value misses its limit at threshold. Deliverable 2 plots this table.
7. Compare the center run with the A08 results and explain every difference.

## Deliverable 2: Sizing plot and cross-plots

1. Draw the sizing plot (slide 5): $W_{TO}$ against $W_{TO}/S$, one curve per $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$ through its three runs, and the three lines of constant $W_{TO}/S$. State the fit. Deliverable 3 draws the constraint lines on it.
2. Cross-plot each constraint against $W_{TO}/S$, one curve per $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$ (slide 6). Draw the limit at threshold and, where the RFP gives one, the limit at objective. Shade the side where the constraint fails at threshold. State the fit.
3. Cross-plot each constraint against $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$, one line per $W_{TO}/S$, in the same way.
4. Read every {term}`crossing`, at threshold and at objective, inside the matrix only (slides 6 and 7). Tabulate the $W_{TO}/S$ of each crossing on a curve and the $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$ of each crossing on a line. For a curve or line with no crossing inside the matrix, enter whether it passes or fails throughout. Deliverable 3 uses this table.

## Deliverable 3: Carpet plot and lightest feasible point

The RFP states: "Design and/or cost trade studies between threshold and objective requirements should be discussed."

1. Draw the {term}`marks <mark>` of each constraint on the sizing plot (slide 7) and join them into its constraint line. State how you draw the constraint line between marks. Tick the failing side. For a constraint whose cross-plot against one of the two variables has no crossing, state how you draw its constraint line.
2. Shade the {term}`feasible region` at threshold. If the matrix has no feasible region, add runs beyond the edge nearest the constraint lines and repeat step 1. If there is still none, name the constraints that exclude each other and stop there.
3. Read the lightest feasible point (slide 8): its $W_{TO}$, $W_{TO}/S$ and $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$. Name the active constraints. Give the margin of every other constraint there, in the units of that constraint, from the fitted matrix. If the point lies on an edge of the matrix, add runs beyond that edge and repeat steps 1 to 3.
4. Run the sizing code at the lightest feasible point. Report $W_{TO}$ and every constraint value. Give the difference between the $W_{TO}$ read and the $W_{TO}$ computed. Check that every limit at threshold is met. If one is not, select a point on its feasible side and run the sizing code again.
5. Offset the curves and draw the carpet plot (slide 9). Show the curves, the lines, the runs, the constraint lines with their ticks, the feasible region and the lightest feasible point. Shift every mark with its curve. State the {term}`offset`. Draw no horizontal axis.

6. Draw the limits at objective as a second set of constraint lines on the carpet plot (slide 13). Read the lightest feasible point that meets them. Report the $W_{TO}$ that each objective value adds when every other limit stays at threshold. If an objective limit leaves no feasible region inside the matrix, add runs beyond the nearest edge once. If there is still none, report that objective as not reached and give its takeoff weight from A08 Deliverable 7 step 3. Never extrapolate.
7. Compare the lightest feasible point at threshold with the A06 design point and the A08 takeoff weight. Explain the differences.

## Deliverable 4: A third design variable

The RFP asks for "the major trade studies conducted justifying the optimization, sizing, architectural arrangement and integration of the specifically selected concept."

1. Choose a third design variable from the first group of Deliverable 1 step 1 (slide 10). State the requirement or the weight it affects and the model of the sizing code that responds to it.
2. Take three values of the variable. One of them is the value in ASORT. Deliverables 1 to 3 are the study at that value. State the spacing.
3. At each of the other two values, run the nine-run matrix with the matrix script, centered as in Deliverable 1. Read the crossings from the fitted matrix. Show one sizing plot with its constraint lines and its lightest feasible point. Run the sizing code again at that point. Cross-plots are not required at these values.
4. Plot the lightest feasible $W_{TO}$ against the variable. State the fit and read its minimum. If the minimum lies at or beyond an end of the range, add values until it lies inside or a limit of the variable ends the range. State which, with the source of any limit.
5. Select the value of the variable. If it is not the minimum, state the reason and the $W_{TO}$ it costs. Enter the value in ASORT. If the value is not one of those already run, run its matrix and run the sizing code again at its lightest feasible point. Draw the carpet plot at the selected value. Deliverables 5 and 6 start from its lightest feasible point.

## Deliverable 5: Sensitivities

The RFP requires that "Quantitative data shall be presented showing why their concept 'works' and is the preferred design compromise that best achieves the design requirements."

1. At the lightest feasible point of Deliverable 4, compute the derivative $\partial W_{TO}/\partial x$ and the sensitivity $s_x$ (slide 12) of the takeoff weight to $W_{TO}/S$ and to $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$, by central difference on the fitted matrix with a step of about 2% of the variable.
2. For each input below, run the sizing code twice, with the input raised and lowered by a step of about 2% of the input (slide 12). Hold $W_{TO}/S$, $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$ and the third variable at the point. Scale the engine as in Deliverable 1. Let the sizing code size the weights and the fuel again. Compute $\partial W_{TO}/\partial x$ and $s_x$.
   - the {term}`thrust specific fuel consumption <TSFC>`
   - the zero-lift drag coefficient $C_{D0}$
   - a factor on the empty weight
   - the crew and payload weight
   - the RFP distance of the mission that sizes the fuel (A08 Deliverable 2 step 5), 600 nm for the ferry flight or the 50 nm cruise leg of the training flight
   - two more inputs that your team considers the most uncertain, with the reason
3. Halve the step for each input and compute $s_x$ again. Report the change.
4. Make a table and a bar chart of the $s_x$ of the inputs of step 2, ranked by $|s_x|$ (slide 12). Report the two $s_x$ of step 1 beside them, not ranked with them. State the point and the step of each $s_x$ in the table.

5. For the three inputs with the largest $|s_x|$, state each uncertainty with its source. Run the sizing code with each input raised and lowered by its uncertainty. Report the change in $W_{TO}$ and in the value of each active constraint, and whether every constraint is still met at threshold.

## Deliverable 6: Design point for PDR

The RFP's data item 1 asks for the minimum weight aircraft.

1. Select as the design point the lightest feasible point of Deliverable 4 or a point with a stated margin to each active constraint. Size each margin from the changes of Deliverable 5 step 5. Report the $W_{TO}$ the margins cost. If A06 selected an engine without scaling it, select the design point on the constraint line of its thrust.
2. Enter $W_{TO}/S$, $T_{SL}/W_{TO}$ and the third variable in ASORT. Run the sizing code to convergence. Run it again on another member's computer.
3. Compute again, at the design point, the rows of the A08 compliance table for the RFP performance requirements and the fuel needed against the fuel carried for the mission that sizes the fuel. Carry the other rows from A08 and mark them as not recomputed. State which objective values the design point meets.
4. Make one slide that records the trade study: the study of Deliverable 1, the carpet plot, the design point, its active constraints and margins, and the three largest input sensitivities.

## Deliverable 7: Defense and reflection

Present the design defense on two slides:

1. The lightest feasible point, its active constraints and the difference between the takeoff weight read and the takeoff weight of the rerun.
2. The objective values the design point meets, and the takeoff weight of each one it does not meet, from Deliverable 3.
3. The third design variable, the value selected, and the change in the lightest feasible takeoff weight from Deliverable 3.
4. The input with the largest sensitivity, its uncertainty and the data that would reduce it.

Any member may be asked to present any slide.

Each member writes a reflection of at most 150 words. Name the result you contributed most to and how you checked it. Name the result you would check first if the A05 engine deck changed. Add one sentence naming the AI tools you used and for what.

## Before you submit

- Every constraint states its limits, weight, altitude, speed and engine rating.
- Every run of every matrix has converged, and the center run is compared with A08.
- Every crossing read lies inside the matrix.
- Every constraint line is ticked on its failing side.
- On the carpet plot every mark is shifted with its curve, and there is no horizontal axis.
- The lightest feasible point has its active constraints, the margin of every other constraint and the rerun.
- Every objective value has the takeoff weight it adds, or is reported as not reached with its takeoff weight from A08.
- The third variable has a matrix and a lightest feasible point at each value, a fit, and its minimum inside the range or a stated limit.
- Every sensitivity states its point, its step and the result with the step halved.
- The three inputs with the largest sensitivity each have an uncertainty with its source.
- The design point, the third variable, the compliance table and the trade study slide agree with ASORT.
- The sizing code has been run again and runs on another member's computer.
- Every AI prompt and response is in the deck appendix.
